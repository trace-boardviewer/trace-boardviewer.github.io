Choosing a boardviewer

TRACE details refer to stable version 1.3.3, published 11 October 2026.

Choose a boardviewer by the files you actually have. This page explains what TRACE covers and where to check other programs. It does not restate the prices, versions or formats of other programs.

Other boardviewers change their prices, platforms, formats and licences. This page makes no claim about them. Check each program's own website or source repository before you choose one. Product names are trademarks of their owners; TRACE is not affiliated with any of them.

What TRACE covers (1.3.3)

  • Price and licence: free, MIT-licensed open source. No account and no telemetry.
  • Windows x64: a portable EXE, not code-signed. Nothing is installed.
  • macOS (Apple silicon, macOS 13 or later) and Linux (x86-64): experimental and unsigned. Native acceptance of 1.3.3 on both platforms is still pending. For 1.3.1, run 37974058845 reported issues on both platforms.
  • Board formats: the generated format table lists every reader, its variants and its limits. Some readers are drafts, and some are checked only with selected real files.
  • Schematics and PDFs: KiCad and EAGLE schematics, Altium SchDoc and PDF datasheets, searched offline, with cross-probing to the board. Scanned PDF pages can be read with bundled English text recognition, started by you for each page, with a limit of 40 megapixels and 120 seconds per page.
  • Notes: per part and per pin, including a voltage or resistance you measured. Notes follow the board file by its content hash, so renaming the file keeps them.
  • Network: an optional update check (on by default, can be turned off) and, only when you choose them, support verification and bug reports. Settings > Network lists each network feature, its host and a request log.

Limits you should know

  • Some readers are drafts or have limited validation. XZZ, HyperLynx, Fabmaster FATF, EasyEDA Standard and CSV/TSV pin lists rely on synthetic checks. Other readers are checked against selected real exports only. Check your file's variant in the format table.
  • Native Allegro has layout limits. Selected documented layouts import parts, nets, pads and outlines. Unknown records, older databases and unverified layer semantics are refused with an explanation.
  • No copper. Tracks, zones and vias are not drawn. Connection guides show logical relationships, not real traces.
  • Experimental builds. The macOS and Linux builds are experimental and unsigned. See the native acceptance note above.
  • Unsigned Windows EXE. SmartScreen may show a warning. The download is about 112 MB. Each launch unpacks the app into a private temporary folder, which is removed on normal exit.
  • Size and format limits. Files are limited to 64 MiB, except native Allegro databases, which open up to 240 MiB when they declare 4,000,000 objects or fewer. Unsupported or damaged files are refused with an explanation rather than guessed.

Other boardviewers

Other free and commercial boardviewers exist, including OpenBoardView, FlexBV, NexusBV, BoardViewer and BoardRipper. Their formats, platforms, licences and prices differ, and they change over time. Their own websites or repositories are the current reference.

Which one fits your work

  • KiCad, EAGLE or Altium designs with their schematics: TRACE reads these natively. The format table gives the exact scope for each.
  • Classic boardview files (.brd, .bdv, .fz, .bvr, XZZ .pcb): check your export variant in the format table. It shows which variants have real-file checks and which are drafts.
  • Allegro or Teboview (TVW) files: TRACE reads selected documented layouts only and refuses unknown ones.
  • Board, schematic, datasheets and your measurements in one window, offline: TRACE.
  • Linux: the .deb and AppImage are experimental. See the Linux page.
