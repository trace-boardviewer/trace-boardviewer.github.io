Boardview software compared: TRACE, OpenBoardView, FlexBV and NexusBV

Last checked: 6 October 2026

If you repair boards, you need a boardviewer that opens the files you actually have and gets you from a fault to a part quickly. This page compares four boardviewers you can use for free today.

TRACE Boardviewer

TRACE is a free, MIT-licensed repair workspace rather than a viewer alone.

You open a board, attach its schematic (KiCad or EAGLE) and any PDF datasheets or photos, and one search covers part references, values, packages and nets across all of them. Board and schematic are linked by exact names: TRACE never guesses a match. Anything that differs, such as a fiducial with no schematic symbol or a net with two names, is listed so that you can confirm an alias yourself. Notes can be attached to a part or a single pin, including the voltage or resistance you measured. They follow the board file by its content hash, so renaming the file keeps them.

TRACE works offline, needs no account and sends no telemetry. Its only network request is an optional update check, which you can switch off.

Best for: KiCad and EAGLE designs and open hardware, and anyone who wants the board, schematic and datasheets in one window, free.

OpenBoardView

OpenBoardView is the open-source boardviewer that describes itself as a drop-in replacement for the Test_Link and Landrex viewers.

It runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. Its source repository has readers for the classic boardview formats (BRD, BRD2, BDV, BVR, ASC, CST and FZ with a key), and the 10.0.0 release notes list GenCAD, XZZ PCB and ASRock CAE support among the additions since version 9. Annotations are stored per board. PDF schematics work through custom builds of SumatraPDF or Evince that its release notes call a beta feature.

Best for: classic boardview files on any operating system, including Linux, in a free open-source tool with a public issue tracker.

FlexBV

FlexBV is a commercial boardviewer with a free edition. Both editions read the same list of boardview and manufacturing formats on Windows, macOS and Linux.

The Professional edition (US$150, perpetual) adds synchronized PDF schematics ("Smart PDF Integration"), KiCad, EAGLE and Allegro boards (its changelog marks them "developing, may crash"; Allegro is listed as beta) and a repair job database.

Best for: repair shops that want PDF schematics tied to the board on Windows, macOS or Linux and are happy to pay for a perpetual licence.

NexusBV

NexusBV is a free boardviewer for Windows that its site describes as under active development. Version 0.9 is dated 20 September 2026. Its format list is broad and includes Allegro (versions 16 to 25) and TVW. Among other features it offers layers, a dual-mode view with two independent views, indexed PDF and boardview search, signal tracing, notes, bookmarks and two-way navigation between board and PDF. A web version runs in the browser; its site says no files are uploaded.

Its website does not say whether the source code is available.

Best for: Windows users who need Allegro or TVW files, or a wide range of classic formats, in a free tool.

Others worth knowing

  • BoardViewer (boardviewer.net): a free Windows viewer, version 2.0.1.9 beta. Its site names extensions such as .asc, .bdv, .brd, .bv, .cad, .cst, .gr, .f2b and .fz. The copyright line on the site reads 2016.
  • BoardRipper (GitHub): an AGPL-3.0 browser-based viewer you can self-host with Docker. It lists fourteen formats, including KiCad, EAGLE and Altium.

Where TRACE is weaker

  • Classic proprietary boardview formats are draft in TRACE. The readers for TestLink/Landrex BRD, TOPTEST BRD2, BDV, FZ/CAE, XZZ, CST, ASC and Samsung CAD exist and are tested against synthetic files. No real vendor file has been tested yet. The other three programs list these formats as core features, and OpenBoardView's open-source readers are the public reference TRACE's readers follow. If a file opens wrongly in TRACE, please report it, sharing only files you are allowed to share.
  • No Allegro and no TVW. Allegro databases are recognized and explained, but not read. TVW is accepted by its extension only, and the content is reported as unrecognized.
  • No copper. TRACE shows parts, pads and nets. Tracks, zones and vias are not drawn, and connection guides show logical relationships, not real traces.
  • Linux and macOS builds are experimental. The Linux .deb and AppImage are x86-64 only and tested automatically on Ubuntu 24.04, not yet on a desktop Linux machine. The macOS build is Apple-silicon only and unsigned, so macOS asks you to allow it once.
  • Unsigned Windows EXE. SmartScreen warns once. The download is about 112 MB, and each launch unpacks about 500 MB into a private temporary folder, which is removed on exit.
  • Limits. Files are limited to 64 MiB. Scanned PDFs are shown but cannot be searched (no OCR). The workspace features added in 1.2.0 (tabs, search, schematic and PDF viewers, notes) are in English whichever of the eight interface languages you choose.

Which one should you use?

  • Mostly classic laptop or phone boardview files (.brd, .bdv, .fz, XZZ .pcb): OpenBoardView, FlexBV and NexusBV read these as core formats, while TRACE's readers are still draft. TRACE is portable, so you can try it alongside them and see whether its schematic and PDF workflow helps you.
  • KiCad or EAGLE designs, open hardware, or your own boards: TRACE reads the board and the schematic natively, free, with exact cross-probing.
  • Linux: OpenBoardView or FlexBV for classic boardview files; TRACE for KiCad and EAGLE designs with their schematics and PDFs (experimental .deb and AppImage). NexusBV also has a browser version.
  • Board, schematic, datasheets and your measurements in one place, offline, at no cost: TRACE.
  • Allegro files: NexusBV, FlexBV Professional (beta) or Cadence's own viewer. TVW (Teboview) files: NexusBV or FlexBV.
